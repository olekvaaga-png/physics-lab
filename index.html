WELCOME TO THE LAB

See what the equations of physics do.

Select an experiment on the left and change it with the parameters on the right. Read “What happens here” under the picture for a plain explanation. In 3D views, drag to rotate and scroll or pinch to zoom; flat 2D views do not rotate. On a keyboard, arrow keys rotate and + / − zoom the focused viewport.

Dimension Explorer projects exact n-cube vertices into 3D. Calabi–Yau Geometry shows a folded mesh section of the Fermat quintic threefold in ℂP⁴, with an optional sample across its six real dimensions. String Vibration Lab models classical waves. Classical Closed Ring compares supported periodic waves with a freely collapsing relativistic loop, and shows the worldsheet swept out in time. Compactification compares momentum and winding on a circle, including their exchange under T-duality. The Section explorer changes fixed coordinates of the quintic.

These models do not establish that string theory describes nature. Extra dimensions and fundamental strings remain unconfirmed experimentally.

Physics experiments

Eighteen physics experiments cover quantum mechanics (superposition, wave packets, measurement and collapse, interference, tunneling, two-level dynamics), atoms and matter (hydrogen orbitals and spectra, crystal bands), oscillators and quantum fields, and the big picture (scales and the status of the evidence). Five string-theory views show extra dimensions, Calabi–Yau geometry, vibrating strings, closed loops and compactification. Every view has a “What happens here” explanation under the picture; flat 2D views are used whenever the content is a graph and not a shape in space.
